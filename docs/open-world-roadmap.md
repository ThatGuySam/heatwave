# Heatwave 99 open-world roadmap

These are the twenty world systems worth building for Heatwave 99. The order
favors interactions that multiply each other. More streets can wait. A witness,
a doorway, and a searching officer already create several different outcomes
on the same block.

| # | Priority | System | What it adds |
| ---: | :---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Live | Deeper police response | Dispatch, pursuit, last-known-position search, roadblocks, and cooldown states the player can read and disrupt. |
| 2 | Live | Roaming civilian NPCs | People walk, talk, flee danger, and react to crimes instead of serving as scenery. |
| 3 | Live | Enterable buildings | Continuous interiors turn streets into hiding places, shops, ambush rooms, and alternate routes. |
| 4 | Live | Witness reporting | A crime creates heat when a civilian or officer sees it, which gives the player time to move before units arrive. |
| 5 | Next | Traffic and driver panic | Drivers yield, swerve, ram, flee, or abandon cars, creating pileups and escape opportunities. |
| 6 | Next | Noise and alarms | Gunfire, crashes, alarms, and sirens pull nearby actors toward or away from a block. |
| 7 | Next | Faction territory | Rival crews patrol their blocks and can collide with police or other crews. |
| 8 | Next | Heatwave pressure | Temperature changes crowd density, vehicle reliability, fire risk, and aggression. |
| 9 | Next | District power grid | Cutting power disables lights, alarms, shutters, signs, and lifts on one block. |
| 10 | Next | Systemic hazards | Fuel, fire, water, gas, and electricity create short chain reactions that change a fight. |
| 11 | Next | NPC tool use | NPCs grab dropped weapons, use phones, steal cars, barricade doors, or fight fires. |
| 12 | Next | Dynamic street incidents | A small director assembles crashes, arguments, robberies, deals, and chases from actors already nearby. |
| 13 | Next | District memory | Recent violence changes patrol density, civilian fear, crew hostility, and shop security when the player returns. |
| 14 | Next | Living businesses | Shops open, close, lock down, lose power, get robbed, and react through their owners. |
| 15 | Later | Emergency services | Firefighters, medics, and tow crews arrive in the world and change traffic, hazards, and sightlines. |
| 16 | Later | Day and night rhythms | Crowd density, open venues, patrol mix, and crew activity change by hour. |
| 17 | Later | Persistent vehicle identity | Plates, alarms, damage, cargo, and wanted status make each stolen car a remembered asset or liability. |
| 18 | Later | Dynamic building occupation | Police, crews, shopkeepers, or squatters reclaim cleared rooms so interiors do not stay solved. |
| 19 | Later | Damage, cleanup, and repair | Wrecks, police tape, burned rooms, and broken lights persist until city crews clear them. |
| 20 | Later | Public transit and crowd flow | Buses move real passengers, while blocked routes redirect people and traffic into nearby streets. |

## Build order

The next pass should combine traffic, noise, and faction territory. A gunshot
would then scare pedestrians, alter traffic, attract police, and pull a local
crew into the same encounter. That is a better use of the current district than
doubling its area.
